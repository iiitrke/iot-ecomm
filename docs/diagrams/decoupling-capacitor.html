IoTEcomm bench notes · 74HC595 at 3.3 V

Where the long wire is, and why the 100 nF sits at the chip

The power supply is far away at the end of long wires. The 100 nF capacitor is a small charged tank a few millimetres from the chip's VCC and GND pins. When the chip switches, the capacitor supplies the burst; the long wire refills it afterwards.

1 · The two current loops

Fast loop: the burst for each switch, from the capacitor (nanoseconds) Slow loop: the supply refills the capacitor through the long wire VCC GND

Follow the green loop: current leaves the capacitor's VCC leg, enters pin 16, passes through the chip, leaves at pin 8, and returns into the capacitor's GND leg. It uses both pins. The shorter this loop, the better, which is why the capacitor goes right next to the chip.

2 · The 74HC595 chip, pin by pin

3.3 V GND Signal from the ESP32 or the chain LED output

Pin numbers run counter-clockwise from the dot: 1–8 along the bottom from left to right, then 9–16 along the top from right to left. That's why VCC (16) and GND (8) end up at opposite corners. On a breadboard, plug the 100 nF capacitor from the pin 16 row to the GND rail right beside the chip, and keep both legs short.

3 · What the chip's VCC pin sees

Illustrative shapes, not a measurement. Dips this short (nanoseconds) are invisible to a multimeter; an oscilloscope shows them. On the left, the dips can push the chip below a clean logic level, so you see flicker or wrong LEDs.
